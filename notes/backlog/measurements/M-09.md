# M-09 measurement: queue scratch charged from kernel metadata (#121)

Status: measured, one boot. The charge is now the kernels' own `private_segment_fixed_size` — a wave
of the heaviest one on every CU, once a card — instead of the flat `n_cu × 5 MiB`. On the served boot
below the plan reads **17.84 MiB** where the flat bound charged **160.00 MiB** (32 CU × 5 MiB), and the
card serves `c1`, `c8` and a 25,414-token `pf20` on it.

GiB between the two trees, which also differ elsewhere, so it is consistent with, not proof of, that
figure.

## What the numbers say, and what they do not

- **Measured:** the charge on a served card is 17.84 MiB, from the kernels' metadata, and the lane
  boots and serves `c1`, `c8`, `pf20` and `greedy` on it; the VRAM rows above are the card's own
  counters before and after each phase.
- **Measured:** what the card holds does not grow past the plan: at the end of the four phases it
  kept 167.0 MiB free, and the two points where it grew are the first kernel of each new code object
  and the first 8-client step.
- **Not measured:** any A/B. No base arm was booted in this session — the lane was handed to the next
  worker before the remaining arms could run, at the coordinator's instruction — so nothing here says
  the reclaimed margin buys speed, and the timing rows above are one boot each with no pair to
  compare against. The `pf20` TTFT in particular was taken while another worker's build was compiling
  on the same host; it is not a performance claim.
- **Not measured:** output parity against the flat-bound arm. The hashes are one arm's.
- **Limitations:** one boot, four phases, one config; the charge is a floor derived from metadata
  (a wave of the heaviest private segment on every CU), not the runtime's own worst case, which the
  header of `core/device/queue_scratch.h` sets out; the measurement above is the evidence that the
  floor is enough for this lane, not a proof for every workload.
